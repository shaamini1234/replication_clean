UK Business Dynamism Database

The creation, life and death of British companies — plus a UK & US large-cap layer. One linked database.

This brings together official public records into a single database that can follow a company from birth (incorporation) through its finances (annual accounts) to its death (insolvency) — for essentially the whole UK company population, and links to the FTSE 100 and S&P 500 for the largest firms. Below: what's in it, then what it already shows.

What it shows — the birth and death of UK firms

Company births per year

New company formation has risen steeply — from ~65,000 a year in 2008 to a peak in 2025. Nearly every company in the database now has a birth year: about half are exact dates looked up from Companies House (with a source link), and the rest are reliable year-estimates derived from the company number — each clearly flagged so evidence-grade dates and estimates never get mixed up.

Company insolvencies per year

The series reproduces real history: low in the mid-2000s, a spike at the 2008 financial crisis, calm in the mid-2010s, then a sharp rise from 2022 — a sign the data reflects reality, not noise.
Validated against official Government statistics. For 2023 our figures match the Insolvency Service almost exactly, type by type: creditors' liquidations 21,159 vs official 20,577; compulsory 2,885 vs 2,827; administrations 1,619 vs 1,567 — all within ~3% (ours runs a little higher because the Gazette covers the whole UK, the official figure is England & Wales only).

Insolvent firms are small and financially exhausted; solvent wind-ups are cash-rich companies closing by choice. Two genuinely different kinds of exit — and 94% of insolvencies are micro or small firms.

The balance sheet hollows out before failure

A typical failing firm's net worth drains from ~£3,250 five years out to almost nothing (£24) in its final year. Insolvency is rarely sudden — the accounts show it coming.

Which industries fail most

Hospitality (restaurants, pubs, takeaways) and construction dominate — the sectors most exposed to thin margins and demand shocks.

Honest limits

Worth stating plainly: insolvency isn't the only way firms close — quiet strike-offs (the larger route) aren't in the insolvency data. Revenue/profit is missing for most small UK firms by law, so their financial picture leans on the balance sheet. Births undercount older years (failed firms leave the register). Work in progress right now is recovering exact birth and dissolution dates for ~250k dead firms, and loading the last 1998–2001 insolvency history. The FTSE/S&P figures shown are from the current working tables and will be reconciled and verified before publication.

Sources: Companies House (register + accounts), The Gazette (insolvencies), FTSE 100 & S&P 500 index data. Single linked database (`business_dynamism`). Build in progress — figures are the current snapshot.
