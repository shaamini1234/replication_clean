**Status:** draft for review. Fill the bracketed fields, send from a

**Where to send:** `info@ftserussell.com`. That is the address printed on

Subject: Permission request — FTSE 100 historic constituent dates for non-commercial research

Dear FTSE Russell,

I am writing to request written permission to use a small extract of FTSE 100
historic constituent data in a non-commercial research publication.

**Who we are.** The Centre for British Progress is a UK research organisation.
We are preparing a study of long-run business dynamism in the United Kingdom,
comparing the composition and turnover of the FTSE 100 with the S&P 500 since
1984. The output will be a free public website and an accompanying research
report. Neither is sold, licensed onward, or used in any product.

**What we would like to use.** Entry and exit dates for FTSE 100 constituents,
taken from *FTSE 100 — Historic Additions and Deletions* (August 2026 edition),
published at
<https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/ftse-russell/en_us/documents/policy-documents/ftse-100-constituent-history.pdf>.
Our dataset holds approximately 420 such dates covering 1984 to 2026.

**How it would appear.** The published outputs are derived statistics — index
turnover rates, the age of companies at index entry, the share of entrants that
are genuinely new businesses rather than corporate reorganisations, and sector
composition over time. We would cite FTSE Russell as the source of the
underlying membership record in the methodology and on the relevant pages.

**What we would not do.** We do not propose to republish the constituent-history
table itself, to offer the dates as a downloadable dataset, or to use the data in
any commercial product, index, or benchmark. If it would help, we are content for
any permission to say so explicitly.

**What we are asking for.**

1. Written confirmation that this use is permitted; and
2. The attribution wording you would like us to carry.

If a licence is required for this use, we would be grateful to know which one and
the terms, so that we can decide accordingly before publication.

We are happy to share the methodology or a preview of the outputs if that would
assist. I can be reached at [email] or [phone].

Yours sincerely,

[Name]
[Title]
Centre for British Progress
[website]

## Notes for the sender

- **Be ready to answer "how many dates".** Roughly 420, across 1984–2026. The
  number is small and it helps the request; don't be vague about it.
- **The 2026 document is public.** That is worth stating — you are asking about
  an already-published policy document, not a licensed feed. It changes who has
  to approve it.
- **If they say a licence is required**, ask for the specific product and the
  non-commercial or academic rate before assuming it is unaffordable. There is no
  public price list, so the only way to learn the number is to ask.
- **If they decline**, the fallback is publishing derived aggregates only and
  citing the public document as a reference rather than reproducing dates. Get
  a view from someone legally qualified before relying on that.
- **Keep the reply.** Whatever comes back is the provenance record for the
  project; file it alongside the dataset documentation.
